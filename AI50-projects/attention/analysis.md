# Analysis

I examined two heads in Layer 8. The diagrams show tendencies in these examples; they do not prove that a head has one fixed grammatical role.

## Layer 8, Head 10 — objects and verbs

In “Maya opened the window before the storm arrived near the [MASK],” the row for `window` places its strongest attention on `opened`. In “Those old trees blocked the road near the [MASK],” the row for `road` strongly attends to `blocked`. These examples suggest that this head can connect a direct object with its verb, even when other words occur between them. The pattern is not universal, and some attention also goes to special tokens or other words.

## Layer 8, Head 11 — modifiers and nouns

In “Those old trees blocked the road near the [MASK],” the rows for `Those` and `old` attend strongly to `trees`; the determiner `the` also attends toward `road`. In “Maya opened the window before the storm arrived near the [MASK],” `the` attends toward the noun that follows it. This suggests a tendency to connect determiners and adjectives with the nouns they modify. The pattern varies across tokens and sentences.

These are qualitative observations from the attention visualizations. Brighter cells represent larger attention weights, but attention weights alone do not establish a head’s complete role in BERT.
